## The two lines

> I’m sorry — I watered the plastic plant on the windowsill every morning for a week while the real fern beside it wilted.
>
> I learned to check for roots before I pour, so now I tap each pot and only water the ones that are actually alive.

## Why this apology

The judge weighs, in order: a robot apology owning one specific mistake with a lesson and a fix; a concrete, human-scale slip; a lesson that follows from that exact mistake and names a changed behaviour; two clean sentences each doing its own job; and a voice like a machine noticing its own slip. The mistake is exact in every detail — the plastic plant, the windowsill, every morning for a week, the real fern wilting beside it — the sort of slip a person would really make, not a vague system failure. The lesson follows from that exact mistake: the robot watered a plant with no roots, so now it checks for roots before it pours and taps each pot first. No generic promise to do better; the fix is a named, changed behaviour.
